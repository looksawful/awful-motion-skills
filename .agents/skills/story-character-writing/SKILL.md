# AWFUL story and character writing

Write for visible behavior. Downstream visual agents cannot render a paragraph of internal explanation.

## Character model

For every major character define:

- external want/current objective;
- internal need or false belief when relevant;
- fear/cost/line they resist crossing;
- capabilities and limits;
- resources and dependencies;
- relationship tensions;
- what they know, do not know and misunderstand;
- behavior under low/medium/high pressure;
- speech rhythm and avoidance/attack habits;
- physical habits or recurring visual actions;
- beginning state;
- ending choice/state;
- visual identity reference handled separately by `character-consistency`.

Backstory belongs in the working model only when it changes current choices or behavior.

## Scene rule

A scene needs:

- a character pursuing something;
- resistance or uncertainty;
- an action/choice;
- a changed state by the end.

If everyone enters and leaves with the same goals, information, relationships and physical state, question whether the scene exists for a reason.

## Write shotable action

Prefer:

- what can be seen;
- what can be heard;
- what can be performed;
- what physically changes.

Convert abstract states into behavior. Do not use dialogue as a dumping ground for lore the characters already know.

## Dialogue

Each important line should do at least one job:

- obtain something;
- conceal something;
- pressure someone;
- redirect;
- test;
- refuse;
- seduce/charm;
- threaten;
- repair;
- reveal accidentally;
- change the power relation.

Characters should not all share the narrator's sentence length, vocabulary and directness.

## Animation short structure

For a short visual piece, compress around:

`setup -> disturbance -> pursuit/escalation -> decisive beat -> consequence/payoff`

This is a functional scaffold, not a mandatory formula. For very short clips, setup and disturbance may be one image; payoff may be one reaction.

## Character-performance handoff

For each important beat, provide downstream motion information:

- intention;
- physical action;
- emotional pressure;
- pose change;
- prop/contact event;
- reaction;
- what should linger or settle.

Then route character animation to `disney-animation-principles` rather than encoding every movement inside dialogue.

## Game narrative

Separate:

- player goal;
- character goal;
- world state;
- gameplay verb;
- narrative consequence.

Narrative should reinforce what the player does. Do not write long cinematic exposition for a mechanic that needs immediate readable feedback.

For NPCs/companions, define state-dependent behavior and dialogue rather than one static biography. Track what events change their relationship/state.

## Continuity state

Maintain only what later scenes need:

- location/time;
- character physical state;
- inventory/props;
- known information;
- relationship state;
- unresolved promise/threat/question;
- visual changes;
- next required consequence.

## Handoff artifacts

Depending on task, produce one or more of:

- character card;
- relationship map;
- beat sheet;
- scene cards;
- screenplay/dialogue scene;
- game encounter spec;
- episode/short outline;
- shotable action pass;
- continuity ledger.

Then route visual planning to `storyboard-and-directing`.

## Anti-slop gate

Reject writing where:

- characters explain themselves instead of acting;
- everyone speaks in polished thematic statements;
- conflict exists only because someone withholds an obvious sentence for no reason;
- every beat escalates with generic "more intense" stakes;
- visual action is replaced by adjectives;
- a character's personality disappears when plot machinery begins;
- the ending resolves without a character choice, action or consequence when the story promised one.
