# AWFUL storyboard and directing

A storyboard is a decision system for staging, continuity and editing. It is not a gallery of unrelated pretty frames.

## Start from beats

Break the scene into observable beats:

- new information;
- decision;
- emotional change;
- physical action;
- reveal;
- reaction;
- transition.

Every shot must earn its existence by carrying at least one necessary beat.

## Scene geography

Before shot design, establish:

- who/what is present;
- entrances/exits;
- important objects;
- screen-left/screen-right relationships;
- eyelines;
- movement axes;
- light direction;
- environment anchors;
- any intentional continuity break.

Use the 180-degree rule as a readability tool, not a religious prohibition. Cross the axis only deliberately and re-establish geography when needed.

## Shot design

For each shot specify:

- shot id;
- narrative purpose;
- framing/shot size;
- camera angle;
- lens language if relevant;
- camera motion;
- subject blocking;
- start/end pose;
- dominant action;
- secondary action;
- duration/hold;
- dialogue/audio cue;
- transition/cut reason;
- continuity notes;
- generation references.

## Visual hierarchy

Stage the eye before adding detail:

1. primary focal subject;
2. supporting action;
3. environment/context;
4. decorative detail.

Use silhouette, contrast, scale, depth, gaze, movement and negative space to control attention. If everything screams, nothing reads.

## Camera rule

Do not move the camera because the video model can. Camera motion should reveal, follow, intensify, destabilize, compare or transition. A static camera is valid when subject performance carries the beat better.

## Performance

For animated characters or expressive objects, read `disney-animation-principles`. Identify anticipation, action, contact/extreme, reaction and settle beats before selecting frames.

For recurring subjects, read `character-consistency` and carry the same master identity references through the shot manifest.

## Storyboard image generation

When generating panels:

- first lock aspect ratio and visual language;
- use persistent character/environment anchors;
- generate panels as shot evidence, not polished posters;
- prioritize pose, blocking, lens/framing and geography over micro-detail;
- include no panel text inside generated imagery unless explicitly needed;
- keep annotations outside the artwork when possible;
- regenerate a panel when continuity is wrong rather than explaining away the wrong image.

Use vendor `inference-storyboard-creation` for detailed shot vocabulary and panel planning when available.

## Animatic preparation

A storyboard becomes animatic-ready when each panel/shot has:

- duration;
- dialogue/VO/SFX cue;
- camera/subject motion note;
- transition;
- order locked enough to evaluate rhythm.

Use holds intentionally. Do not assign identical durations to every panel out of convenience.

## Continuity ledger

Track across shots:

- character costume/state/injuries/props;
- object positions;
- entering/exiting direction;
- gaze;
- hand holding a prop;
- time/weather/light state;
- camera side of axis;
- action phase at cut;
- emotional state;
- effects that persist or disappear.

## QA questions

A shot list fails if:

- two adjacent shots communicate the same thing without useful emphasis;
- geography becomes accidentally confusing;
- a cut breaks action direction without intent;
- characters teleport or swap props;
- camera motion has no visual/narrative purpose;
- generated panels mutate identity/style;
- a crucial beat exists only in explanatory text and not in a visible/audible action;
- the sequence cannot be timed into an animatic without inventing missing beats.
